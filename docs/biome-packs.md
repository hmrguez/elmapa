# Biome packs

Reference audit, 21 November 2026.

A biome pack should make its environment recognizable through the combination of ground, vegetation, water and relief. A map of a desert needs sparse plants and flat-topped relief as well as warm ground. A map above the treeline needs low vegetation and exposed rock even where it has little snow. Start with Desert and Alpine to exercise both warm and cool palettes, sparse and dense vegetation, and different relief silhouettes. Reuse the existing Woodland materials as the temperate baseline.

## Authoring behavior

The brush Orbit shows Core, Desert, and Alpine for Land, Water, Forest, Mountains, and River. Choose a pack, then a finish for the current brush. River groups channel colors using the Water finishes, and Core also offers the original channel color. Classic and Woodland control atlas-wide pigments. Landmark artwork is browsed through the Landmark Orbit and its own packs.

Define each biome in the [biome pack manifest](../assets/biome-packs/manifest.json), with a stable ID, display name, and references to brush materials. For each brush, list the representative finish first. Reuse an artwork ID where packs share a finish. The biome catalog also lists complementary landmark IDs for discovery through MCP. Keep authored geometry and texture choices usable across the editor, shelf, and SVG.

## Art priorities

1. **Desert and Alpine.** Extend Desert with gravel pavement, a pale salt-flat surface, and a caravan stop. Extend Alpine with low shrubs or lichen above the treeline, loose gray scree, patchy snow and crags with exposed snow faces. A cabin and a pass cairn would add useful landmarks to the Alpine set.
2. **Coastal Wetlands and Tropical Rainforest.** Add mudflat and lagoon materials, reed clusters for cooler salt marsh, and rooted mangrove silhouettes for warm coasts. Add distinctly layered broadleaf crowns for rainforest, with taller trees and breaks around clearings and water. Reuse the reef, sea arch, mangrove, waterfall and cenote landmarks where appropriate.
3. **Prairie, Mediterranean Scrub, and Boreal Forest and Tundra.** Build a low tussock family for prairie, rounded dry scrub and open oak stands for Mediterranean terrain, and spaced spruce grading into dwarf willow, sedge and lichen for boreal and tundra maps. Distinguish the tundra surface from Alpine with muted low relief and more peatland and pond choices.
4. **Hydrology and specialty relief.** A dry wash should read as exposed sediment in a channel, with intermittent edge marks and little or no water fill. Give braided meltwater parallel branches and exposed bars, and reserve narrow branching entrances for tidal creeks. The existing brushes can approximate these forms in a composed map. A dedicated line finish or geometry is a follow-up when a single stroke needs to express one of these forms. Give volcanic environments separate silhouettes for shields, cinder cones and caldera rims, plus a dark, low-contrast lava ground finish.

Author vegetation with spacing and scale appropriate to each family and position its ground anchor consistently across specimens. Continue using seamless Land and Water tiles and bottom-aligned relief frames. Distinguish salt flats and perennial lakes through texture and edge treatment. ## Readability checks

- Give adjacent environments distinct ground, vegetation or relief silhouettes. Keep texture grain weaker than shorelines, routes and letterforms. Natural Earth’s region-sensitive shading shows why lowland colors should vary with environment, and ColorBrewer explains why categorical colors should preserve the ability to distinguish categories. [Natural Earth elevation tints](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/10m-raster-data/10m-cross-blend-hypso/) · [ColorBrewer scheme types](https://colorbrewer2.org/learnmore/schemes_full.html)
- Use a quiet, light road bed with a darker shoulder and differentiated river banks. Check the same label over pale sand, snow, dark vegetation and water. Inspect an overview at shelf size and an edit close-up in grayscale as well as color. [Elmapa road art direction](path-art-direction.md)
- Build one small map per first pack and one mixed map. Include two land finishes, water with a shoreline, overlapping vegetation and relief, a road, a text label and a placed landmark. Compare the swatches, live painting, settled canvas, reopened atlas, shelf preview and SVG. Open a saved Classic and Woodland map to check existing artwork and color behavior, and exercise a missing pack or material reference using an isolated atlas library.

Use original artwork for new assets. If adapting a source texture, verify its redistribution terms and record the source in the brush credits. [Existing texture credits](../assets/brush-materials/README.md#texture-sources)
